SUMO v9 implementation and verified pilot notes

This is an independent microscopic extension, not a literal replication of the v8 mesoscopic source. SUMO 1.28.0 runs through libsumo in-process. Models are synthetic and not calibrated to a corridor.

API
- buildnetwork(root=None): compile v7 nodes/edges to a SUMO network with normal physical internal intersection connections and protected one-incoming-approach signal stages.
- make_scenario(seed, load, missions, warmup=1800, horizon=7200, mission_end=5400): returns fixed ordinary release/class/route and ambulance mission schedules; policy does not enter generation.
- run_case(seed, load, missions, policy, output=..., **overrides): returns a dict including history ndarray and per-mission rows; logs JSON, NPZ exhaustive partition trace and individual ordinary trip-event CSV. Run this in separate processes for concurrency; libsumo is not thread-safe.
- CLI accepts --seed --load --missions --policy --warmup --horizon --mission-end --harm-budget --epsilon --base {fixed,longest_queue} --step {1,.5,.25} --output.

Policies
P0 uses count-derived traffic-aware dynamic routing and no signal priority.
P1 selects routes without anticipating signal priority, then requests unconditional preemption.
P2 selects routes anticipating aggressive preemption (30 s lookahead, maximum green60 s); same receiving-capacity physical simulation as every policy.
P3 enumerates up to four loopless route continuations crossed with actions none, (10 s lookahead,20 s green cap),(20,40),(30,60). It chooses predicted ETA within epsilon of the predicted fastest, then smallest surrogate ordinary burden. Route switches need >10 s predicted gain against incumbent. Entry into downstream edges triggers replanning. This is an approximate joint policy, not globally optimal routing.

Causal observations
Every five seconds, each link supplies a count sample with bounded {-2,-1,0,0,0,1,2} noise and two-second receipt delay. Sensor availability is a Markov process: available-to-missing probability.015, missing-to-available.25. Namespaced draws are the same across paired policies; the true counts differ under interventions. No future plant schedule is passed to controller. Historical assigned mean flows and nominal design X are public assumptions. Queue estimate is max(0,observedlinkcount minus historicalvehicleflow times freeflowtime), not physical true queue. No actual field detector exists in this study.

Joint surrogate
For each candidate route, sum freeflow travel, estimated queue-clearance and nominal cyclic residual delay, allowing anticipated preemption reductions. For conflicting approach j and estimated lostgreen D, ordinary burden is qhat_j D + historicalLambda_j D^2/[2 max(1-X_j,.05)]. This is only an isolated-queue-inspired ranking surrogate. It is not a bound on network cost and does not account exactly for spillback, partial turns, observer state, future signal phases or actual heterogeneous discharge headways.
At the next junction, priority is admissible if predicted time benefit >=5 s, predicted burden <= harm_budget, and downstream observedcount <.85 times coarse CSV storage. Legal switch delay includes remaining minimum green/amber/allred/pedestrian interval. Predicted crossing within a holdcap uses lookahead, queueclear and a two-second start-up allowance. Once admitted a P3 request is committed until the ambulance leaves the incoming edge, the selected actual green-time cap expires,120 s since admission passes, or headroom becomes inadequate; the service-age guard may override. There is no automatic renewal on the same incoming edge after expiry. Earliest-dispatched ambulance wins conflicting requests at a junction. Estimated benefits are not guarantees, and request/prediction failure is a measurable outcome.

Signals and physical scope
Each approach is protected separately; ordinary movements obey SUMO car following and internal junction geometry. Eight-second minimum green, three-second amber and two-second allred are preserved. A25-second allred pedestrian service opportunity is scheduled, but no physical pedestrian agents or crossing geometry are modeled. The guard prioritizes an observed occupied approach or pedestrian service whose red/service age reaches150 s. It is not a per-vehicle waiting-time guarantee. Longest-queue comparison is explicitly not max-pressure. All policies share the same base guard and command timing. Ambulances have no speed privilege, no siren red-light bypass and no rescue-lane yielding device in the primary study. Mixed vehicle classes are simulated with heterogeneous dimensions and car-following parameters; there is no calibrated sublane filtering model.

Load and stochastic inputs
Nominal load is an ex ante PCU capacity surrogate using assumed saturation flows and a Webster-inspired bounded cycle/split design, not achieved SUMO saturation. The network and ordinary routing geometry comes from v7 CSVs. Up to4 near-shortest routes per OD are logit-weighted. Ordinary arrivals use a shared stationary AR(1) lognormal minute shock (rho .75; innovationSD .18), Poisson event times quantized upward onto integer seconds; class and route draws use a separate namespaced stream. All new microscopic scenarios start empty. Warmup is an initialization period, not demonstrated stationarity. No incident or yielding sensitivity is included in this module yet; v8 mesoscopic source can supply those separate analyses.

Metrics and verification
At every physical simulation time step, an exhaustive ordinary-only partition has59 regular edge groups, one internaljunction group and8 desired-but-not-inserted origin queues. Sum equals desiredreleased minus completedordinary exactly. Ambulances are subtracted everywhere. Integrating these left-endpoint piecewise-constant counts gives exactly the sum of individual restricted residence intervals under recorded simulation clocks; both identities raise errors on a discrepancy. All desired releases are integer-grid and actual completion/observed-insertion clocks use SUMO getTime(), so .5/.25 steps do not round events to an outer one-second controller tick. Ordinary events CSV exports the independent individual-residence audit. Positive spatial excess A is dt * sum(max(history_policy-history_P0,0)); signed deltaJ is dt * sum(history_policy-history_P0). They have vehicle-second units, and A >= max(deltaJ,0) on this exhaustive fixed partition. Desired-but-uninserted ambulances remain censored, with travel restricted at the common horizon. No vehicle is silently removed; time-to-teleport is disabled. Colliding/teleport vehicle events and all completed signal timing intervals are counted.

Progress guard extension after the initial training failure
The original44ced source is preserved byte-for-byte as sumo_study_initial.py. The current module adds optional progress_guard_s=None,20,10,5 without changing unguarded behavior. Tests on the requested seed5001/X.9/M4 cases reproduced the original training P0/P2/P3(no guard) mean travel times exactly.

For ambulance a at an observed regular edge e and longitudinal position x_a(t), define executed free-flow progress
P_a(t) = sum over previously executed regular edges f of L_f/v_f + max(0,x_a(t))/v_e.
Only actually entered and completed edges are accumulated. Future route edges do not enter P. Internal connectors contribute zero free-flow allowance; the guard is not evaluated while the sampled vehicle is on an internal edge, and their elapsed time can therefore contribute to excess delay on the next regular-edge observation. Initial front-position offsets and sampled connector transitions are part of this declared progress proxy.
Define D_a(t)=max(0,t−desired_dispatch_a−P_a(t)). This includes waiting to insert because the clock begins at desired dispatch; the first physical observation can immediately trigger if insertion was delayed. For threshold gamma, z_a is initially0 and updates irreversibly to1 when D_a(t)>gamma. If z_a=0, ambulance a uses P3 joint selective selection and admission. If z_a=1, it uses P2 joint time-oriented routing and strong30s/60s aggressive priority for the rest of its mission. A newly triggered ambulance replans immediately even in the middle of its current regular link, clears its own current discretionary lease and thereafter bypasses P3 harm/headroom admission gates exactly as the P2 comparison policy does, while remaining subject to physical receiving capacity and the common service-age guard and clearances. Mode is passed explicitly through route scoring, plan selection and request admission; the controller's policy for other ambulances is never temporarily mutated. Simultaneous requests still use earliest-dispatch arbitration.

The threshold is a causal measured-progress escalation rule, not a guarantee that P3 travel is within gamma of P2: P2 may have pre-cleared a junction before the threshold is observed, interventions can change later traffic, and route choices can already have committed different physical paths. The global predicted-time band applies while P3 is active; after escalation the rule is the P2 comparator with its original switching hysteresis. Fallback counts and each trigger's ambulanceID, time, desireddispatch, observed excess delay and executedfreeflow progress are exported. Sourcefuture input arrays remain absent from controller state. Tags include progress_guard_s to prevent overwriting unguarded outputs.
