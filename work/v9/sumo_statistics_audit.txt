Independent review of microscopic experiment statistics
25 October 2026

Scope
Read-only review of work/v9/analyze_sumo.py and work/v9/run_sumo_batch.py while the main experiment was running under the frozen protocol. No simulator, batch-runner or analysis source was edited. The only manuscript edit was to paper_theory.txt, limiting Proposition3 and its explanatory text explicitly to selective-mode planning before progress-triggered fallback. Final variable normalization remains with the parent.

Overall finding
The planned main inference correctly uses paired scenario seeds as independent blocks. Its principal cohort, spatial-exposure and individual-residence calculations are coherent. No blocking arithmetic error was identified in Dplus, the episode-level Clopper-Pearson intervals, the seed-aggregated bootstrap or the intended numerical-step difference of contrasts. One runtime statistic is potentially misleadingly named and should be relabeled or recomputed from the available whole-episode timing denominator before publication. Some additional assertions would strengthen analysis verification without changing simulations.

Paired matching and completeness
The main protocol has48 seeds, three background-load settings, three scheduled mission counts and four policies, giving1728 runs and432 matched scenario cells. analyze_sumo constructs an index by(seed,load,missions,policy), asserts the expected total and unique-key count, and then accesses all expected combinations. Missing expected keys therefore fail loudly. Within every cell it checks identical route-schedule hashes, ambulance ID/dispatch/origin tuples and ordinary cohort size. The runner also asserts that each policy shares P0's scenario hash and individual ordinary ID set.

These checks support matched comparisons within cells. The use of policy-independent desired releases and route schedules, source queues and fixed observation windows is important: blocked insertion cannot improve a controller by deleting a traveler. The model and runner hashes and exact per-run configurations must accompany the final records. Main records retain their actual configurations, so these can be checked against the frozen protocol after execution.

Independent unit and aggregate estimand
Each cell vector is ordered by the protocol's seed list before policy subtraction. Bootstrap draws therefore resample paired episode differences rather than independently resampling two policies. Mission-level fractions and mean durations are first computed inside an episode, which preserves interaction among ambulances sharing traffic. The aggregate comparison first averages the nine cell differences within each seed and then bootstraps the48 seed averages. This correctly preserves dependence induced by reusing one seed across the background-load and mission-count settings.

The aggregate estimand weights each tested(load,missioncount)cell equally. It is not a distribution-weighted estimate for a real city and is not a mission-weighted mean over all pooled ambulance journeys: a one-mission cell and a ten-mission cell receive equal aggregate weight. The text should state that weighting explicitly. The432 scenario cells and all repeated policy trajectories are descriptive experiment counts; the aggregate interval uses48 independent seed blocks, not432 or the total number of mission records.

Bootstrap and claim scope
The percentile procedure uses10000 resampled seed vectors and a deterministic hash-based random seed for each reported contrast. This is reproducible and gives approximate pointwise95 percent intervals. The procedure does not claim exact finite-sample coverage, simultaneous coverage across all cells/outcomes, or immunity to heavy-tailed/small-sample effects. No p-values are calculated here, so the earlier v8 sign-flip concern does not apply to these new bootstrap estimates.

The training criterion selects parameters whose P3-minus-P2 episode-mean ambulance difference is no more than20 s, then minimizes positive spatial exposure. That training condition is not a held-out guarantee. A held-out pooled ambulance noninferiority statement needs the prespecified upper confidence bound below20 s. A pooled positive-exposure benefit needs its P3-minus-P2 upper bound below zero. Requiring both directional statements is an intersection-union compound claim; it does not require independence of the two endpoints. The current two-sided95 percent intervals provide conservative one-sided upper bounds if used that way. If either condition fails, the compound objective has not been demonstrated at that precision. A point estimate below the margin alone is insufficient.

Any cell-specific claim requires its own interval and should be labeled exploratory or handled within a prespecified multiplicity scheme. Passing a pooled margin does not establish the margin in every traffic setting or for every ambulance. The20 s progress threshold and the20 s evaluation margin are also different quantities, despite sharing a numerical value in the chosen configuration. Neither is a clinical threshold or a real-world relative-time guarantee.

Restricted and tail ambulance outcomes
The runner retains every mission, with a censored duration restricted at the absolute experiment end. It additionally computes the average of min(recordedtravel,600) and the fraction exceeding300 or600 s, counting nonarrivals as failures. Under the current protocol the last dispatch occurs no later than mission_end5400 and the experiment ends at7200, leaving at least1800 s. Therefore every censored mission has more than600 s of observable follow-up, so these tail classifications and the fixed600 s restricted mean are valid for this protocol. The same code would not identify a600 s exceedance for arbitrary future protocols with less than600 s available follow-up; add an assertion of that condition when analyzing main records.

The any_mission_over_600s indicator is one Bernoulli event per seed within a fixed cell and policy. Clopper-Pearson intervals correctly use48 such independent episode indicators, rather than treating multiple correlated missions as independent binomial trials. The stored pair of beta-quantile endpoints is the standard two-sided95 percent interval. The interval estimates the probability that an episode has at least one late/noncompleted mission at that mission count. It is not an individual-mission risk interval and it changes interpretation as mission count changes. Even with zero observed episode failures,48 independent trials give a two-sided upper endpoint of about7.4 percent, not zero. The bootstrap mission-fraction outcome is a separate seed-clustered estimate.

Individual traveler outcome
The batch worker reads each archived ordinary-event CSV and constructs the ID-to-restricted-residence mapping. P0 supplies the common ID reference; each policy uses exactly that ID set. Dplus is sum(max(j_policy-j_P0,0)) and Dminus is sum(max(j_P0-j_policy,0)). These formulas are correct, preserve individual identity and do not cancel a harmed traveler against a helped traveler. The worker checks that their signed sum equals the spatial-ledger signed residence difference to numerical tolerance. analyze_sumo independently repeats the Dplus-minus-Dminus identity check in main records.

The event files derive j from the same common desired release times, observed simulator completion clocks and fixed warmup/end window as the occupancy ledger. They include unfinished journeys and source waiting. Dplus therefore measures positive individual RESTRICTED residence difference. It is not automatically full-trip delay. It is correctly distinguished from positive68-group spatial exposure, which can change with ordering and location. Both refer to P0, so comparing P3's Dplus with P2's Dplus is a comparison of their positive effects relative to that common P0 counterfactual. It is not sum(max(j_P3-j_P2,0)); a direct P3-versus-P2 individual-harm measure would be another estimand.

Spatial outcome
The runner subtracts P0's entire recorded partition trace, uses the correct physical step for integration, and verifies signed decomposition into positive and negative exposure. All policies in one cell share the exhaustive68-group partition. The raw count traces are converted to int32 before subtraction, avoiding subtraction overflow at the current synthetic magnitudes. The final analysis expresses positive exposure, Dplus and signed residence in vehicle-hours by dividing vehicle-seconds by3600. Each quantity remains an episode outcome with source waiting and unfinished ordinary travel retained.

Sensitivity and ablation contrasts
For the smaller-step sensitivity, each smaller-step P3 and P2 outcome is computed relative to that step's own P0 where relevant. Analysis then forms(P3-P2)_quarterstep-(P3-P2)_halfstep for the same seed and cell. This is the correct difference of treatment contrasts for diagnosing step sensitivity. It is not simply a comparison of raw P3 outcomes, and any description should preserve that distinction. The same principle applies to spatial and individual metrics whose P0 reference itself changes with numerical step.

For the progress ablation, the analysis forms unguardedP3-minus-guardedP3 at the same half-step seed and cell. Its P0 background is unchanged. This estimates the effect of removing the progress guard within the chosen remaining settings; it does not isolate every possible fallback design. Twelve-seed sensitivity intervals can be wide, and a zero-containing interval does not prove numerical equivalence or that the guard is irrelevant.

Recommended analysis-only hardening
Before publishing, assert that each sensitivity/ablation file has the count and keys declared by its own protocol, that no duplicate keys are silently overwritten in its dictionary, and that its paired schedule hashes match within phase and the corresponding main scenario. Assert identical spatial partition order and physical step within every main pair, and at least600 s follow-up for each desired mission. Assert that the sum of every event CSV equals the run's ordinary residence and that event IDs are unique before constructing a dictionary. Most of these properties are already enforced upstream; repeating compact checks at the analysis boundary documents their provenance. These additions require no simulator changes or traffic reruns.

Runtime reporting issue
controller_wall_time_s in sumo_study.py times request/planning plus signal updates at ALL outer control ticks, including warmup and periods without a live ambulance. analyze_sumo divides that total by active_decision_ticks to produce controller_total_per_active_tick_ms and episode_average_controller_ms_p95. These are amortizations of the whole controller cost onto active ticks, not measured latency of an actual active ambulance decision. Labeling them as decision time or latency would be inaccurate. Available records permit total controller compute divided by the total number of outer one-second control ticks, and distribution of per-episode average whole-controller cost per tick. Active-only latency cannot be reconstructed without new instrumentation, which is unnecessary for this paper if the existing metric is named honestly.

Similarly, wall_time_s starts around SUMO start and ends after close, before later trace/event export; prior scenario generation, network compilation and public-model preparation are outside it. It is a simulator-loop runtime measure, not complete end-to-end system deployment latency. n_mission_trips sums scheduled missions over all policy replays. Call this mission evaluations; independent scenario/seed counts must remain separate.

No manuscript conclusion in this audit is based on opening completed main effect estimates. The main experiment was still running during this source review, so passing or failing the scientific targets must be determined from the final locked analysis.
