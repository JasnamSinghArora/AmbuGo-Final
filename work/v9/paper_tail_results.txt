## Condition heterogeneity and long trips

The balanced result does not hold with the same precision in every condition. At nominal load 0.95 and one mission, JSEL minus JFAST was 17.80 s [9.74, 27.17], and spatial exposure changed by −1.27 vehicle-hours [−3.04, 0.52]. At the same load with four missions, the time difference was 16.17 s [10.83, 22.84]. These upper time endpoints exceed the 20 s target even though the balanced-grid upper endpoint does not. The one-mission spatial interval also includes a small increase. These condition-specific intervals are descriptive and do not establish simultaneous coverage.

S0 and SEQ each left one of their 2,160 scheduled mission evaluations unfinished, in the same seed-2, load-0.95, ten-mission episode. That mission was observed for 2,579 s from desired dispatch before the common horizon. JFAST and JSEL completed all 2,160 missions. The number exceeding 600 s was 106 for S0, four for SEQ, five for JFAST and two for JSEL, counting the unfinished missions as exceedances. These are descriptive counts across correlated missions and heterogeneous conditions, not independent binomial samples or evidence of zero operational risk. In a single 48-episode cell, even zero late episodes has a two-sided 95 percent binomial upper endpoint of approximately 7.4 percent.

The two JSEL trips exceeding 600 s lasted 1,812.5 and 2,050 s. Both occurred at nominal load 0.95 in seed 2, at doses four and ten respectively. Their insertion delays were only 0.5 s, so their long durations were not simply external admission waiting. The first escalated to aggressive priority at time 3,476 s, yet its subsequent regular-link entry records had a gap of 1,589 s. At the start of that gap, its computed remaining ETA was 276.8 s; actual remaining travel was 1,718.5 s. These logs establish a large forecast error and prolonged on-network delay. They do not identify a unique physical cause from the aggregate traces alone.

This case exposes the limits of count-derived queue proxies, approximate downstream signal forecasts and entry-triggered route replanning. After its one progress-triggered escalation, the controller normally replans only upon entry to another regular link. A vehicle that remains on its current link can therefore retain an outdated continuation for a long period. Revisiting the route while stalled, forecasting receiving-space dynamics and diagnosing blocked lane connections are concrete follow-up requirements. The frozen evaluation retains these failures rather than repairing the algorithm after examining held-out outcomes.

The 600 s restricted-duration sensitivity gives balanced means of 390.98 s for S0, 213.73 s for SEQ, 210.12 s for JFAST and 221.25 s for JSEL. It limits the numerical influence of very long trips but does not remove their operational importance. Reporting the common-horizon mean, fixed-window mean, exceedances and noncompletion together avoids attributing reliability to a favorable average alone.

The shorter 300 s threshold reveals an additional cost of selectivity. The balanced fraction of missions exceeding 300 s increased by 2.55 percentage points under JSEL relative to JFAST, with a 95 percent seed-block interval of [1.42, 3.77] percentage points. The corresponding difference for the 600 s fraction was approximately −0.069 percentage points [−0.185, 0.000], based on very few extreme events. These outcomes do not establish a general reliability improvement. The primary target concerns a mean-time tolerance and spatial exposure; it does not preserve every deadline measure.

A post-analysis influence diagnostic recomputed the balanced effect after excluding each seed in turn, without changing the primary results. Across the 48 leave-one-seed-out means, the time difference ranged from 10.61 to 11.40 s, the spatial-exposure difference from −4.04 to −3.67 vehicle-hours and the positive individual-residence difference from −1.72 to −1.53 vehicle-hours. The pooled point estimates therefore do not depend on retaining any single seed. These ranges are sensitivity summaries, not confidence intervals or evidence that rare-event uncertainty has been resolved; no seed was removed from the reported main analysis.
