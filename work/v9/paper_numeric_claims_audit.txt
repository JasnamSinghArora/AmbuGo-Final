Necessary wording corrections and qualifications

1. Common-horizon restricted duration must appear in the abstract. S0 and SEQ each contain one unfinished ambulance, both seed2,nominalX.95,M10,ambulancea007. Their recorded2579 s values are lower restrictions at the shared end, not observed completed travel times. All JFAST and JSEL missions complete, so their duration means coincide with completed travel means in this archive. Calling all four aggregate means unrestricted ambulance travel time obscures that distinction. Prefer balanced common-horizon restricted ambulance duration, with completed-duration and fixed600 s sensitivity reported separately. The results opening already acknowledges unfinished missions, but the abstract needs the same precise metric label.

2. The pooled criterion does not hold at the stated precision in every cell. At nominalX.95,M1, the JSEL-minus-JFAST duration interval is[9.74,27.17] s and the spatial interval is[-3.04,+.52] vehicle-hours; neither component supports that cell's compound criterion. At nominalX.95,M4, duration is[10.83,22.84] s, so the20 s margin is unresolved despite a spatial improvement. This does not invalidate the pooled result, but the paper must explicitly limit that result to its balanced aggregate.

3. The shorter deadline has a measurable penalty. Balanced JSEL-minus-JFAST probability of a mission duration above300 s rises by0.025463, or2.55 percentage points, with a95 percent seed-block interval of[1.42,3.77]percentage points. This is a material secondary tradeoff. A mean time tolerance and lower congestion do not preserve every ambulance reliability endpoint. It should be discussed alongside rare extreme trips rather than reporting only the favorable means.

4. Extreme missions remain. JSEL has two completed trips above600 s,1812.5 s and2050.0 s, both at seed2 in distinct high-load mission-count settings. JFAST has five above600 s, with maximum2400.5 s; S0 has106 including its censored trip; SEQ has four including its censored trip. Each policy has2160 mission evaluations, but these are correlated within seeds and do not provide2160 independent binomial trials. JSEL's two extreme trips demonstrate directly that the20 s pooled criterion and20 s progress threshold are not per-mission guarantees.

5. Rare-risk improvement is not established. The balanced above600 s fraction contrast is-.000694 with interval[-.001852,0]. It is based on very few affected seed episodes and has a boundary at zero. Do not describe this as a proven risk reduction or use zero-event cells as zero risk. Episode-level exact intervals and seed-clustered fraction intervals have different estimands and should be labeled.

6. One comparative phrase is inaccurate. The results draft says the main conclusion uses independent evaluation seeds, not the more favorable development point estimates. For the selected configuration, development duration gap17.56 s and spatial change approximately-1.06 vehicle-hours were actually LESS favorable than main11.10 s and-3.84 vehicle-hours. Remove more favorable; the scientific point is independence from the training outcomes, not their ordering.

7. Negative contrasts retain the common S0 reference. The draft correctly states that JSEL-minus-JFAST Dplus is the difference between their positive traveler effects relative to S0, not the sum of positive direct JSEL-minus-JFAST individual effects. Keep this sentence. Similarly, a spatial-exposure reduction does not itself establish that every ordinary traveler is less delayed.

Secondary numerical-step claims
The two reported quarter-step-minus-half-step differences of JSEL-minus-JFAST contrasts match sumo_analysis.json to rounding. Low nominal load: duration+.30[-.06,.74] s, spatial-.25[-2.30,1.41]vehicle-hours and individual-.03[-.67,.48]vehicle-hours. High nominal load: duration-23.27[-79.12,7.76] s, spatial-1.45[-7.12,5.10]vehicle-hours and individual-2.68[-7.02,.36]vehicle-hours. Each uses12 paired seed blocks. The high-load intervals are wide and do not establish numerical equivalence; the draft already warns that intervals containing zero do not imply equivalence. The fixed600 s duration sensitivity is less dominated by rare long trips and may be useful alongside the unrestricted-follow-up diagnostic, while remaining secondary.

Base-controller, observation and no-switch secondary sections were still being extended when this audit was requested. Their final numbers and exact phase counts should be checked after those batches finish. This audit approves only the numerical claims actually enumerated above and their stated source snapshot.

Final assessment
The primary synthetic aggregate findings are numerically reproducible and the favorable pooled mean/exposure tradeoff is supported by the prespecified approximate interval rule. The principal remaining scientific risks are external validity, local forecast misspecification, heterogeneous cell effects, the shorter-deadline penalty and rare long missions. The abstract appropriately denies global city optimality and field validation; retain those boundaries while correcting the restricted-duration label and reporting the unfavorable reliability outcomes.
