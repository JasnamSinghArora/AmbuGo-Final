## Causal progress guard

The initial selective policy can preserve a narrow predicted time band while substantially delaying the realized ambulance: its count-based queue proxy and local pulse model do not reproduce the microscopic queue exactly. We therefore evaluate an explicit causal escalation rule. For ambulance a observed on regular edge e at position x_a(t), let

$$P_a(t)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal E_a^{\mathrm{completed}}(t)}L_f/v_f+\max(0,x_a(t))/v_e,\qquad D_a(t)=[t-d_a-P_a(t)]_+,$$

where d_a is desired dispatch. The completed-edge sequence contains executed travel only, including any actually followed route changes; future route edges are excluded. Waiting to insert is included in elapsed time. Internal connectors receive no free-flow credit, and evaluation resumes at the next regular-edge observation. This is a progress proxy, not a calibrated travel-time oracle.

Each ambulance has an individual binary mode z_a, initially zero. At the one-second decision times, z_a changes irreversibly to one if D_a exceeds the selected threshold gamma. Before escalation, P3 applies its joint route–action selection, predicted-time band and burden-selective priority. Upon escalation it immediately replans from its current edge using P2's joint time-oriented route selection and strong 30 s look-ahead/60 s hold request, then uses that policy for its remaining mission. Its own priority lease is cleared. Other active ambulances retain their own modes, and the common service-age guard, request arbitration and physical clearances remain in force. We evaluate gamma in {5,10,20} s against no escalation using development episodes before freezing the final policy.

The progress guard supplies a measurable trigger rather than a relative travel-time guarantee. Its threshold is excess over an executed free-flow proxy, whereas the comparator has a different evolving traffic history and may pre-clear intersections. Consequently escalation at gamma does not imply that final ambulance travel differs from P2 by at most gamma. The finite predicted-time-band assertion applies only before escalation; the subsequent mode is explicitly the P2 comparator. Fallback counts and trigger-specific delay, time, progress and ambulance identifier are recorded. This extension was developed after an initial training failure; all such development episodes remain outside held-out inference.
