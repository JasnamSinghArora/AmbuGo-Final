AMB UGO FIRST DRAFT THEORETICAL AND STATISTICAL FOUNDATION
25 October 2026
This file supplies paper-ready definitions and arguments plus a critical audit of the supplied v8 report. It contains no invented experimental outcomes. Numerical results must be inserted only from the new archived runs. Candidate controller details must match the actual implementation rather than the other way round.

1 Scope and relationship to existing work
Emergency routing and signal control have already been studied together. Hosseinzadeh, Sinopoli, Kolmanovsky and Baruah formulate emergency-centered multi-intersection model predictive control, using a traffic-state model and signal configurations, with centralized and decentralized variants [R1]. Thus a paper cannot claim that combining ambulance routing with signal priority is itself new. Capacity-sensitive signal control is also established: capacity-aware back-pressure control explicitly treats limited storage and congestion propagation [R2], while classic max-pressure results assume a store-and-forward queue network and simplifying arrival, turning and capacity conditions [R3]. Those stability results must not be transferred to a finite-storage SUMO network by naming the policy max-pressure.

The defensible first-draft contribution is a reproducible implementation and a controlled synthetic experiment examining a specific selective, load-sensitive joint route-and-priority rule, with both signed network residence and positive spatial congestion burden measured against a persistent paired baseline, across ambulance demand and background loading. Its originality is a candidate contribution requiring a fuller nearest-method comparison, rather than a claim of firstness. The necessary empirical question is whether selectivity offers a useful ambulance-versus-background tradeoff under the stated simulator assumptions, not whether greater mathematical vocabulary improves the system.

Microsimulation is useful because vehicle length, car following, lane assignment, turning and junction interactions affect ambulance passage and spillback. SUMO supplies a microscopic traffic simulator and programmable signal control [R4,R5]. Default microscopic parameters are not locally calibrated traffic behavior. Official FHWA guidance distinguishes model error checking from fitting observed time-varying conditions and testing alternatives [R6,R7]. A successful synthetic implementation is therefore an internally verified computational study; calibration against a real corridor remains a separate task requiring observed signal, count, trajectory and emergency-trip data.

2 Causal system definition
Let G=(V,E) be a directed road network, with directed lanes, legal turn connections and a finite physical storage region on each link. The complete microscopic state X_k includes all live vehicle positions, speeds, lane identities, routes and classes; controller stages and elapsed times; pending departures; signal commitments; and any incident state. External demand and behavior innovations are Z_k. A simulator step is X_(k+1)=F(X_k,U_k,Z_k). A controller receives an information set I_k and chooses U_k=pi(I_k). I_k may contain observations received by k, current ambulance telemetry, already acknowledged commands and public network geometry. It may not contain future realized vehicle arrivals, future incident draws, or the paired baseline's future trajectory.

Route choice and priority are coupled because the travel cost of an ambulance route depends on the legal signal interventions available on it, while the congestion cost of an intervention depends on the route's conflicting approaches and receiving space. A rolling decision therefore chooses a route or route continuation r and signal intervention a together, or applies a declared approximation to that joint problem. The term optimal is always relative to a specified candidate set, cost model and forecast. Dijkstra's algorithm on observed travel-time weights returns a shortest path for those additive frozen weights; it does not prove a globally optimal route under endogenous signal changes, spillback and unknown future demand. A load-aware heuristic should be described as a heuristic until an exhaustive or mathematically justified reference comparison establishes its finite-domain regret.

For ambulance mission m, let d_m be its desired dispatch time and e_m^pi its arrival time under policy pi. The clock begins at d_m even if insertion is delayed. With fixed mission restriction L, define R_m^pi=min(e_m^pi-d_m,L), taking e=+infinity for nonarrival, and report the nonarrival or deadline-exceedance indicator separately. A restricted duration alone can hide failure and must not replace the failure ledger. Simultaneous or successive missions within a replication share a network and are statistically dependent.

3 Exact finite-horizon residence accounting
Fix a follow-up interval [t0,H], a cohort of ordinary vehicles with desired release times a_i common to every policy, and exit times e_i^pi (possibly infinite). Every released traveler remains in the accounting ledger while waiting outside the modeled network for insertion. Define I_i^pi(t)=1{a_i<=t<e_i^pi}. The total ordinary accumulation is N^pi(t)=sum_i I_i^pi(t). Then the restricted ordinary residence is

J_H^pi = integral_(t0)^H N^pi(t)dt
        = sum_i [min(e_i^pi,H)-max(a_i,t0)]_+.

Proof. Substitute the indicator representation of N, interchange the finite sum and integral, and note that each indicator integrates to the length of [a_i,e_i^pi) intersected with [t0,H]. No stationarity, equilibrium, queue distribution, Little's law or complete trip completion is required.

The paired ordinary effect is Delta J_H=J_H^pi-J_H^0, where 0 is a common no-priority baseline. This measures the difference in restricted residence. Calling it a change in total delay is justified only with an explicit policy-invariant free-flow reference and a cohort/horizon convention under which the reference subtraction cancels. With unfinished trips, the safest label is restricted ordinary residence, retaining units vehicle-seconds or vehicle-hours. Use the same desired departures, ordinary route realizations, incident realizations and fixed horizon for each policy. A vehicle excluded because insertion was blocked would falsely improve a congested controller.

For a time-stepped implementation, sum the counts actually sampled using the declared left- or right-endpoint rectangle rule. The count integral and vehicle-level ledger should agree under the same discrete accounting convention; discrepancies up to an arbitrary number of steps are not proof of correctness. Prefer an exact step ledger based on desired releases and actual completions, plus an independently accumulated occupancy check. All live vehicles on SUMO internal junction edges must be counted. A partition based only on ordinary road-edge counts misses those vehicles.

4 Positive spatial burden and its limits
A signed network effect can conceal spatially displaced congestion. Define a fixed exhaustive spatial partition B containing the regular road links, internal junction regions and source waiting regions. Let n_b^pi(t) and n_b^0(t) be ordinary vehicle accumulations in group b under treatment and baseline. Define
A_H^pi = integral_(t0)^H sum_(b in B) [n_b^pi(t)-n_b^0(t)]_+ dt,
B_H^pi = integral_(t0)^H sum_(b in B) [n_b^0(t)-n_b^pi(t)]_+ dt.

Proposition 1. Delta J_H=A_H^pi-B_H^pi, hence A_H^pi>=max(Delta J_H,0).
Proof. Apply x=[x]_+-[-x]_+ to each paired group difference, sum over the exhaustive partition and integrate. Exhaustiveness and unique assignment are essential: every ordinary live or externally waiting vehicle must belong to one group exactly once.

A_H records the accumulation of positive spatial differences and deliberately prevents a queue reduction elsewhere from canceling a local burden. It does not identify the delay of individual travelers and depends on the fixed spatial partition. Different vehicle ordering under two policies can produce persistent spatial differences even when aggregate network accumulation is close. Report signed Delta J_H, A_H and terminal imbalance together, and retain a partition sensitivity where feasible. A positive A_H is a measure of spatially displaced occupancy, not proof that every affected traveler is harmed.

Proposition 2. If a coarser partition is formed solely by merging groups, its positive burden is no larger than the fine-partition burden.
Proof. For any collection of real differences d_b, [sum_b d_b]_+ <= sum_b [d_b]_+. Apply this to each merged group and integrate. Consequently comparisons across different spatial resolutions must not be interpreted as controller improvements.

The baseline is a persistent counterfactual trajectory with the same external scenario and its own lawful control evolution. Resetting its queues to the treated network after each control step erases accumulated effects and changes the estimand. In a simulator both potential trajectories can be generated under a structural model. On real streets only one is observed, so the counterfactual requires experimental design or model-assisted causal assumptions.

5 Queue conservation, storage and spillback
For a group or link i, conservation gives n_i(k+1)=n_i(k)+f_in,i(k)-f_out,i(k), with nonnegative transfers. A physically admissible node allocation limits outgoing transfers by available sending vehicles and incoming transfers by receiving storage. In a packet model, vehicle count and PCU are distinct conserved ledgers: PCU weights are capacity approximations rather than literal traveler counts, and fractional PCU storage cannot replace physical collision checking. SUMO separately represents physical length and minimum gaps. Do not require it to preserve a PCU-derived capacity equality that is only an analytical design input.

For constant unimpeded inflow lambda, outflow mu and initial occupancy n0<K, if lambda>mu, the storage-fill time is (K-n0)/(lambda-mu), until another constraint changes either rate. This is a local diagnostic, not a citywide traffic theorem. Receiving occupancy, remaining storage and the expected number of vehicles released by a priority interval should therefore enter a selective priority rule. A green signal cannot create downstream space; prioritizing a blocked outgoing link can waste conflicting service while moving no ambulance.

The macroscale literature motivates monitoring total accumulation and spatial heterogeneity. Geroliminis and Daganzo's Yokohama findings support useful aggregate flow-density relations under their measured conditions [R8]; subsequent work relates urban network capacity to structural characteristics [R9]. Those findings do not establish a universal macroscopic fundamental diagram for this synthetic network. Do not estimate a critical density from three demand settings and label it a universal congestion threshold. The experiment should report its measured densities, source waiting and completion rates; local occupancy and receiving headroom are more directly relevant to a particular ambulance maneuver than a single network mean.

6 What inverse slack can and cannot establish
Consider an isolated fluid queue initially empty, constant arrival rate lambda and service mu>lambda. The baseline serves immediately at rate mu. An intervention removes all service for a duration Delta and then restores it. During interruption q(t)=lambda t. Afterwards the queue is q(Delta+s)=[lambda Delta-(mu-lambda)s]_+. Thus the clearing time after service restoration is lambda Delta/(mu-lambda), and the total additional queue area is
C = lambda Delta^2/2 + (lambda Delta)^2/[2(mu-lambda)]
  = lambda mu Delta^2/[2(mu-lambda)]
  = lambda Delta^2/[2(1-X)], X=lambda/mu.

This formula is exact for the stated isolated fluid model. It reveals inverse slack as lambda approaches mu, but it is not the same as assuming that every network intervention loses mu Delta vehicles or that a single global X controls all queues. A signalized baseline may itself have a red phase and an existing queue. Both baseline and intervention can discharge at saturation for an interval, in which case their relative backlog does not decrease at the nominal mu-lambda rate until one regime changes. Finite storage, arrivals blocked upstream, changed routes, offset shifts, amber discharge and multiple missions further change the area. A k/(1-X) fit to network means is a candidate empirical relationship to compare against alternatives; a high R-squared over a few chosen loads is not proof of a universal law.

A more general paired recovery statement avoids that mistake. Let d(t)=q^pi(t)-q^0(t) for a common population, with d(t0)=D>0. Conservation gives d'=Delta lambda-Delta mu. If relative net depletion Delta mu-Delta lambda <= r(t), where r(t)>=0, then
[d(t0+s)]_+ >= [D-integral_0^s r(t0+v)dv]_+.
Proof. Integrate d'>=-r from t0 and use the monotonicity of the positive-part map. If r is a positive constant, return to zero cannot occur before D/r, and the post-t0 positive area is at least D^2/(2r). This is conditional on a valid bound on relative net depletion. It supplies a mechanism and diagnostic bound, rather than a generic predictor for microsimulation recovery.

7 Protection definitions and causal signal safety
The signal automaton must specify the active approach or compatible movement set, elapsed green, minimum green, amber, all-red, pedestrian commitments and maximum extension. A priority request cannot directly turn a conflicting red green while skipping required transitions. SUMO documents that direct signal-state control leaves transition management to the application [R5]; merely calling the API is not a safety proof. A verifier should reject conflicting greens and shortened amber/all-red and record each actual stage transition.

A maximum gap between service opportunities, a pedestrian-stage age and a queued vehicle's waiting time are different objects. Serving an approach before its age reaches 180 s does not guarantee that every queued vehicle crosses within 180 s: the queue may exceed the number that can depart in one service interval. An earliest-deadline stage guard is a specific scheduling algorithm and is not a proof that all possible feasible deadline schedules are found. Observed violations under that algorithm demonstrate violations of that implementation, not structural infeasibility of every controller.

A true impossibility argument needs a demand-bound or capacity condition. For example, if vehicles with deadlines in an interval require more conflict-free service time than is available after mandatory clearance and other commitments, no schedule can meet every deadline in that interval. The synthetic study can instead report violation count, excess wait and change relative to a common guarded baseline; these remain empirical outcomes. Hard signal legality must be maintained, while operational waiting targets should be labeled design targets rather than promises until feasibility is established.

8 Statistical estimands and independent experimental units
The independent unit is one complete scenario seed: a common demand realization, incident realization, ordinary route set and ambulance schedule. All policies are evaluated on the same seed, and differences are formed within seed. Multiple missions per hour are dependent within a scenario; treating them as independent observations artificially narrows intervals. Primary ambulance endpoints should be computed as an episode-level mean restricted mission duration with a separate episode failure count or indicator. Report mission distributions descriptively and use seed-block resampling for inference.

The primary selective-versus-aggressive comparison asks whether selectivity reduces positive burden while preserving a prespecified ambulance-time tolerance; route-only and no-priority policies provide secondary reference points. A route-aware aggressive comparator must use the same route options, information, amber/all-red rules and relevant limits. Otherwise a comparison bundles route quality, signal authority and selectivity. A sequential route-then-priority comparator should choose the route independently of candidate signal interventions; a method optimizing signals over every candidate route is already joint.

Policy parameters must be fixed from a separate development set before confirmatory seeds are analyzed. The new computation is a first synthetic study and need not pretend to be preregistered in a public registry; an archived protocol timestamp and code hash truthfully document the internal lock. Any design change made after inspecting pilot results should be logged and all later claims should use separately held-out seeds. If the limited compute budget yields wide uncertainty, report that precision limit rather than repeatedly adding replications until significance appears.

Use seed-level paired means and percentile-bootstrap intervals, resampling complete seeds, with the number of resamples and random seed recorded. Student-t intervals for paired differences provide a sensitivity check; their exact finite-sample validity requires normal differences, while bootstrap coverage is approximate. A paired sign-flip test is exact only under a justified label-exchangeability or sign-symmetry null, not merely because seeds are paired. A weak null E[D]=0 does not by itself make arbitrary sign reversal valid. If sign-flip values are reported, state that assumption and treat them as a sensitivity diagnostic unless the design supplies the randomization justification.

For families of secondary comparisons, Holm adjustment controls familywise error provided its input p-values are valid. It cannot rescue invalid sign-flip assumptions or post-selection tests. Report effect sizes and intervals even when p-values are uninformative. A confidence interval containing zero is compatible with both benefit and harm and does not establish equality, zero cost, or noninferiority. A practical no-harm claim requires a prespecified meaningful margin and a corresponding one-sided or equivalence/noninferiority analysis.

Exact Clopper-Pearson intervals require independent Bernoulli trials. With multiple correlated missions per episode, apply them only to an independent episode-level event such as any mission failure, or use seed-cluster resampling for pooled mission risk. With zero events in n independent scenarios, the one-sided 95 percent upper bound is 1-0.05^(1/n). Zero events is not zero population risk. Two-sided intervals have a different upper endpoint; report which convention is used.

The subsequently supplied v8 package contains all 14,400 evaluation rows: 6 loads x 2 yielding settings x 6 policies x 200 seeds. An independent read-only audit reconstructed all 72 main-table rows, all 48 contrast rows, the 60 Holm-adjusted delay tests and the 72 tail-count records to reported rounding. Its statistics are arithmetically coherent, subject to the scientific and inferential qualifications documented separately. Selected traffic-trajectory reproduction remains a separate code-to-results check. The paper must distinguish the archived mesoscopic study from newly executed SUMO extensions and report the actual counts of each.

9 Recovery, finite horizons and several missions
A fixed horizon is necessary for unbiased policy comparison but right-censors recovery. An increasing signed cumulative effect at H does not prove it will remain increasing: later negative differences can reduce the net integral. Thus the observed signed cost is not automatically a lower bound on eventual total cost. By definition positive burden A_H is monotone as H grows for the same paired trajectories and fixed partition, so its finite-horizon value is a lower bound on that same infinite-horizon positive-burden functional if it exists; it is not a lower bound on signed total delay.

For mission episodes define recovery only after the last ambulance has cleared or arrived and after all priority holds have expired. A concrete recovery diagnostic is the first time all registered paired occupancy thresholds and nominal-controller conditions hold for a fixed dwell interval. Failed recovery before H is right-censored. Under continuing random demand two paired networks may retain vehicle-order differences, so exact state equality is unnecessarily strict and should not be silently equated with no remaining causal effect. Report both per-seed thresholds and ensemble signed/positive trajectories.

The several-ambulance experiment should vary a declared mission process or a fixed count over a defined dispatch window. If counts 1,4,10 are fixed over one hour, call them scheduled mission counts per hour rather than draws from a Poisson ambulance process. Keep hospital destinations and origin weights explicit; overlapping service requests require a deterministic arbitration rule, such as earliest predicted ambulance arrival with stable ID ties, so outcomes do not depend on iteration order. Several missions share the aftereffect ledger: dividing total network burden by the scheduled count gives an episode-level average, not the isolated causal cost of each mission. Nonlinear interaction can be quantified by comparing burden at several counts against the count-scaled one-mission burden under matched scenarios, with uncertainty clustered by seed.

10 Information value, yielding and interpretation
The performance difference between a noisy-state controller and the same formula fed perfect current queues is a within-policy sensitivity experiment. It is not a universal upper bound on the value of any estimator or any information-using controller: a fixed imperfect rule may fail to exploit the extra information, or even perform worse. A decision-theoretic value-of-perfect-information bound instead compares appropriately optimized policies under nested information sets using the same objective and admissible decisions. The new paper should therefore describe any perfect-state test narrowly and retain simple observation models unless their extra complexity demonstrates a practical gain.

Similarly, a one-second signal perturbation is an actual causal intervention in the simulated system. Its effect distribution diagnoses sensitivity to signal timing and paired trajectory divergence; it is not automatically measurement noise or a universal statistical noise floor. The replication requirement follows from the variance and effect size of the actual prespecified paired comparison. A pilot variance can guide a fixed follow-up sample size, but a requirement of hundreds cannot be universal across all networks and metrics.

Yielding parameters are behavior assumptions. If yielding has a smaller marginal ambulance benefit when preemption is active, that pattern may indicate partial substitution; it does not establish that the interventions are complements. Interaction should be defined using a matched factorial contrast. With ambulance time as a loss, define I=(T_preempt,yield-T_preempt,noyield)-(T_nopreempt,yield-T_nopreempt,noyield); its sign describes the change in yielding's marginal effect in that model, with a paired confidence interval. Real Indian mixed traffic requires observed lateral trajectories, usable road width, vehicle class and siren-response behavior; PCU weights alone do not calibrate lateral ambulance passage.

11 Corridor data and what is genuinely still required
A credible real-corridor calibration package contains synchronized lane and turn geometry; controller stage, amber/all-red and override logs; per-movement counts in time bins; discharge headways from queued service; queue-length or occupancy traces; travel times and trajectories; desired ambulance dispatch, entry and arrival times; and intervention triggers and acknowledgements. Yielding requires video or trajectories locating lateral space and driver response, not merely an ambulance travel-time mean. Missing logs should be acknowledged rather than supplied by the synthetic generator.

Split measured days or episodes into fitting and independent validation blocks. Calibrate demand and turning first, then bottleneck throughput and signal behavior, then car-following/lane-changing/yielding behavior consistent with the intended interventions. Match time-varying queue and travel-time envelopes rather than only total hourly throughput [R6,R7]. A scalar count-fit statistic does not validate intervention response. Compare the predicted effect of observed overrides where such data exist, accounting for the fact that operators intervene under atypical traffic. Until these data exist, all claims concern the declared synthetic scenario and tested simulator rather than government deployment performance or clinical response improvement.

12 Suggested manuscript claim discipline
Acceptable: 'We implement and evaluate a selective route-and-priority controller in a synthetic SUMO district, report paired episode effects across background loading and scheduled ambulance demand, and audit the vehicle and signal accounting.'
Conditional on actual results: 'Under the tested assumptions, selective control reduced positive spatial burden by ... while the ambulance-time difference relative to aggressive priority was ... [interval].'
Unacceptable before evidence: 'The optimal city route is guaranteed'; 'Priority is free below X=.7'; 'The network obeys a universal inverse-slack law'; 'Microsimulation validates the city'; 'Observed guard failures prove every controller infeasible'; 'A six-second fixed-policy information contrast bounds all estimator value'; 'SUMO verification proves safe real deployment.'
