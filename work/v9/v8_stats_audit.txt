Independent audit of the supplied AmbuGo v8 statistics and scientific claims
25 October 2026

Scope and evidence
The supplied package at work/v9/teammate_v8/ambugo_v8 was read without edits. This audit independently reconstructed the statistical tables from archived per-run CSV rows. It did not rerun all 14,400 traffic trajectories; traffic reproduction and the new SUMO extensions are separate work by the parent. The reproducible audit script is work/v9/audit_v8_statistics.py and its machine-readable output is work/v9/v8_statistics_audit.json.

What checks out
The evaluation file contains exactly 14,400 rows: six nominal target loads, two yielding settings, six policies and 200 replications, giving 72 complete cells. Each cell contains every replication 0 through 199 exactly once. There are no duplicate cell/replication keys. Dispatch time, ambulance origin and incident-presence labels agree across paired policies. All ambulance durations are finite in this archive; the largest is 861.2025 s. The archive records zero gridlock flags under its particular gridlock detector. All signed ordinary paired effects equal the policy residence minus the corresponding baseline residence to within 5.4e-14 vehicle-hours.

All 72 main-result rows and all 48 policy-contrast rows were independently reconstructed. Their ambulance means, reported paired percentile-bootstrap coordinates and waiting-summary means agree to the documented four- or three-decimal rounding. The 60 delay sign-flip p-values and their Holm adjustment also reproduce to the reported precision. All 72 tail counts match, and the Clopper-Pearson interval endpoints agree to within 5e-5. The stored analysis therefore has coherent raw rows and correctly implemented arithmetic for its stated bootstrap, sign-flip, Holm and binomial procedures. Whether those procedures justify the claims is a separate question.

The auxiliary archives contain 1,760 tuning rows, 2,400 information-sensitivity rows and 450 signal-nudge rows. The tuning code uses replications 5000–5039, while main evaluation uses 0–199. This separation is implemented and reduces direct reuse of tuning outcomes for evaluation. It does not independently establish that the analysis was publicly preregistered or that all model choices preceded evaluation.

The inverse-slack fit is numerically reproduced: fitting the six no-yield P2 mean ordinary-residence changes to k/(1-X) gives k=0.07612996 and centered in-sample R-squared=0.90480. That is an empirical fit, not proof of the law. The fitted costs at nominal X=.60 and .70 are approximately .190 and .254 vehicle-hours, while the observed means are -.029 and -.002. A three-parameter quadratic fits the same six points with R-squared=.98698 and a two-parameter inverse-plus-intercept model gives .92287. Extra parameters improve in-sample fit and are not evidence that the quadratic is true. The useful conclusion is that six selected means do not identify a unique network scaling law. A proper mechanism comparison needs uncertainty weighting, a preregistered model family and out-of-sample or cross-validated assessment.

Corrections needed before using this evidence in a paper

1. Residence is mislabeled as delay. scripts/run_experiments.py defines delay_vh as the sum of all ordinary accumulation N from dispatch to horizon divided by 3600. This is restricted ordinary residence, including travel and external waiting. The paired difference is a rigorous finite-horizon occupancy-accounting effect. It is not established by Little's law and should not be called total delay without a precise policy-invariant free-flow subtraction and censoring convention. Person-hours use synthetic occupancies and inherit the same residence interpretation.

2. No statistical difference is not no cost. The low-load confidence intervals include both signs, so the text must say no clear signed-residence effect was detected at this precision. It cannot say the true cost is zero or that priority is free. Similarly, failure to reject P2-P1 delay differences does not establish equal cost or noninferiority. A declared practical margin and a one-sided or equivalence analysis are needed for that stronger claim.

3. Sign-flip null assumptions are missing. ambugo/stats.py labels its sign-flip test a test of mean difference zero. Independent sign reversal is exact under a suitable exchangeability or sign-symmetry null; an arbitrary mean-zero paired-effect distribution need not satisfy it. Pairing common seeds does not provide randomized assignment of method labels. The numerical p-values reproduce, but exactness for the stated weak null is unsupported. Report paired bootstrap effect intervals and, if retained, label sign-flip values as a symmetry-dependent sensitivity test. Holm correction is computed correctly but cannot repair invalid input-test assumptions.

4. Finite signed cost is not automatically a lower bound. Future negative paired accumulation can reduce the cumulative signed effect. A still-increasing curve at the observation end supports right-censored or unresolved recovery in this model, not a theorem that eventual signed delay must be greater. Positive spatial burden, defined as an integral of positive paired group differences with a fixed partition, is monotone with horizon and has a different legitimate lower-bound interpretation. The v8 archive only contains network-level signed curves and cannot retrospectively identify the full positive spatial burden without new runs or saved spatial trajectories.

5. Current-state sensitivity is not a universal value-of-information bound. Feeding true queues to the same P3 rule estimates that rule's sensitivity to its input, not the performance of the best controller under perfect information. A different controller may exploit information more effectively. The result does not justify a universal six-second ceiling or invalidate all Bayesian estimation. It can justify prioritizing a simpler estimator within this particular policy implementation until extra computation demonstrates benefit.

6. Timing perturbation is an intervention. The 'placebo' changes a signal duration, so it is not a zero-treatment placebo. Its outcome distribution diagnoses intervention sensitivity and propagation in this simulator. It cannot by itself define a universal noise floor, prove chaotic dynamics, or establish a necessary replication count of order 100. Replication needs should follow the paired variance and meaningful effect of the actual comparison.

7. Guard failure does not prove structural infeasibility. n_wait_over increments each time an ordinary vehicle discharges after a stop-line wait above 180 s. Thus it counts over-threshold stop-line discharge events, not necessarily distinct vehicles, and excludes waits of vehicles still queued at the horizon. The guard's service opportunity and pedestrian-age mechanisms do not guarantee every vehicle crosses within a deadline. A breached threshold under one earliest-deadline guard does not prove no feasible controller exists. Report this metric accurately and add unfinished-wait exposure if making waiting-protection claims.

8. Storage has an explicit ambulance exception. Simulator.has_space returns true unconditionally for the ambulance. Ambulance insertion also bypasses ordinary external admission and begins 20 m down the origin link. The audit permits storage plus the ambulance's PCU when the ambulance is present. Consequently 'storage never exceeded' literally overstates the model's invariant: ordinary occupancy is limited while an ambulance reserve of its own PCU is allowed. This should be disclosed or corrected and rerun. The main mesoscopic benefit estimates should not be portrayed as validated physical mixed-traffic predictions.

9. Ambulance service uses an exception. At a green/amber discharge the ambulance is allowed to leave even without sufficient PCU credit and does not consume that credit. This exception is shared by the policies, but it remains an explicit service advantage beyond an ordinary PCU vehicle, despite the broad statement that the ambulance has no speed privilege. It can affect capacity accounting and must be described. The new SUMO extension should retain actual car-following, storage and signal compliance so this simplification is challenged independently.

10. Exit storage is abstracted away. Vehicles entering an exit link are immediately removed from its physical deque and scheduled for later completion; their remaining exit travel is counted through the completion ledger. This preserves the ordinary network count ledger but exit links cannot queue or spill back. The model therefore has finite storage on internal links, not a uniformly physical finite-storage simulation of every link.

11. Rho is not a calibrated Indian yielding model. Reordering FIFO queues after independent class-weighted yielding decisions is a declared mesoscopic abstraction. It does not model lateral space, lane changes, physical pull-aside trajectories or collisions. A smaller marginal yielding gain under preemption is compatible with substitution; the report's blanket 'complements' description requires an explicit factorial interaction definition and analysis. The yield RNG also advances at policy-dependent events; although its seed is shared, realized random numbers are not keyed to an invariant vehicle-event identity across policies. This weakens a claim that the same individual yielding realizations are held fixed, while common demand and sensor draws remain well defined.

12. The v7 oversaturation count conflicts with the archived comparator. The report says eight approaches were oversaturated; the package's stored v7_vs_v8_baseline.json reports n_X_over_1=4 and maximum X=1.319 for both guarded and pure fixed-time v7-plan versions. The associated design.py docstring claims eight and X up to 1.66. These are inconsistent retained narratives, likely from earlier versions, and the final paper must use the recomputed current design values and distinguish guarded from pure fixed-time comparisons.

13. T3/T4 are analytical consistency checks, not external city validation. The Webster comparison tests one isolated-junction queue regime; it subtracts a stated .5 s discretization correction and uses a 15 percent acceptance tolerance. The lost-green comparison uses one deterministic arrival family and a 20 percent tolerance. Their agreement is useful model verification, but no observed corridor or ambulance trajectory is tested. T5 is a rough inverse-slack consistency check derived from those four fluid cases rather than an independent proof of a universal network law. More detailed runtime collision/signal constraints remain separate.

14. Several important claims need narrower language. 'Queues are the binding constraint above X=.8,' 'the disturbance is absorbed,' and 'skipping is the lever that matters' are interpretations of this particular model and action set. The alternative route and priority library differs from the much larger v7 proposal; it is not a completed implementation or empirical test of CCR. Neither a 200-seed table nor the new SUMO cross-check will establish global city-route optimality. Claimed controller runtime must identify exactly what work is timed; the v8 short decision benchmarks cannot stand in for the full estimator, communications and command-checking pipeline that v7 proposed.

Publication use
The 14,400-row archive is statistically coherent and useful as a large, auditable synthetic mesoscopic study, with all requested limitations made explicit. It is reasonable to use its reanalyzed effect estimates as model-relative evidence, after independent selected-trajectory reproduction confirms the source-to-row path. The new load-sensitive/multi-ambulance SUMO experiment supplies a more physical and directly relevant extension; agreement or disagreement should be reported, not forced. Retain the v8 model as a distinct experimental layer, rather than labeling the entire paper verified real-world traffic science. No field-calibrated deployment or clinical benefit has yet been established.
