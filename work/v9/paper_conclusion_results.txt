## What the observed tradeoff establishes

The held-out primary experiment supports a modest average improvement in the time–aftereffect trade-off within this synthetic district. Relative to JFAST, JSEL was about 5.3 percent slower in balanced mean ambulance duration, while positive spatial exposure was about 5.8 percent lower and positive individual restricted-residence increase about 6.9 percent lower. Relative to S0, JSEL still created a positive signed ordinary-residence cost of 2.63 vehicle-hours per balanced episode. Selective priority therefore reduced a cost of aggressive intervention; it did not remove ordinary-traffic costs or demonstrate a globally minimal aftereffect.

The empirical benefit is also partly a hybrid-control result: approximately half of JSEL missions switched to aggressive control. Its interpretation should include that switch, the finite route library, the forecast assumptions and the base signal plan. A short label such as “AI-optimized traffic control” would obscure the actual mechanism and overstate what was tested. The accurate claim is a reproducible joint-routing and selective-priority heuristic with measured average benefits and material residual tail failures.

The mathematical additions strengthen definitions and verification rather than proving a new global traffic optimum. Conservation identities and positive-part decompositions are elementary; the isolated-queue expression is exact only under its stated assumptions; and the finite time-band result concerns computed forecast values. The algorithmic contribution is an explicit executable integration whose benefits can be tested and whose failures are retained. A publication claim should be positioned accordingly, with direct comparisons to stronger joint optimizers, calibrated intervention response and independent network tests still required.

The secondary experiments leave substantial uncertainty about numerical and operating-condition robustness. At nominal load 0.95, halving the physical step changed the selective-minus-aggressive time contrast by −23.27 s, with a wide interval [−79.12, 7.76]. At that load, changing to a longest-count base rule changed the spatial-exposure contrast by +10.19 vehicle-hours [−0.21, 22.84]. These comparisons do not establish equivalence across simulation steps or base controllers. The large intervals and long-trip cases require a larger, deliberately targeted gridlock and numerical-sensitivity study before treating the observed balanced benefit as a robust operating guarantee.
